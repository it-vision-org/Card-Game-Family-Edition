# Mobile Card Game — Architecture and Database Design Request

I want to develop a private mobile card game for three family members:

* Ahmed
* Rahma, Ahmed’s fiancée
* Mamti, Rahma’s mother

Ahmed and Mamti have a close mother-and-son relationship, even though she is Rahma’s mother.

The working title of the game is **“Between the Three of Us”**.

## Project objective

The purpose of the application is to create enjoyable, emotional, funny, and memorable moments between the three players.

The game is not primarily competitive. It should help the players:

* Learn new things about each other.
* Remember shared experiences.
* Have fun and laugh together.
* Express appreciation and affection.
* Discuss their future as a family.
* Strengthen the relationship between Ahmed, Rahma, and Mamti.

## Chosen technologies

I currently plan to use:

* **Flutter** for the mobile application.
* **Spring Boot** for the backend API.

Please evaluate whether this is an appropriate choice and recommend the complete technology stack required for a secure, maintainable, and scalable application.

The application will initially be used by only three people, but the architecture should make it possible to transform it later into a public game where other families or groups can create their own accounts and game rooms.

## Core game content

The game contains approximately **150 cards** divided into:

* Around 135 question cards.
* Around 15 challenge or surprise cards.
* Power cards that players can use during a game session.
* Game rules.
* An optional scoring system.
* A golden rule explaining the emotional purpose of the game.

## Question categories

The question cards are divided into categories such as:

### How Well Do We Know Each Other?

Questions about personalities, preferences, habits, emotions, gifts, reactions, and how well the players understand one another.

### Memories and Stories

Questions about:

* First impressions.
* Childhood memories.
* Family occasions.
* Shared meals and outings.
* Meaningful moments.
* Funny situations.
* Important events in the players’ relationship.

### Fun and Guessing

Questions where players guess who is most likely to perform a certain action or behave in a certain way.

Examples include:

* Who is most likely to get lost?
* Who takes the longest to choose an outfit?
* Who is most likely to laugh during an argument?
* Who is most likely to forget something while travelling?

### From the Heart — Love and Honesty

More emotional questions about:

* Appreciation.
* Trust.
* Emotional needs.
* Fears.
* Reconciliation.
* Support.
* Love.
* Promises.
* Maintaining the family relationship after marriage.

### The Future and Family

Questions about:

* Life after marriage.
* Family traditions.
* Future trips.
* Weekly family activities.
* Celebrations.
* The relationship between Ahmed and Mamti in the future.
* How Rahma wants the relationship between her mother and husband to develop.

## Card targeting

Not every card is necessarily answered in the same way.

A card may target:

* One specific player.
* Two players.
* All three players.
* A specific relationship, such as Ahmed and Mamti.
* A specific relationship, such as Rahma and Mamti.
* A specific relationship, such as Ahmed and Rahma.
* Any player selected randomly.
* The player whose turn is currently active.

The data model should support these different targeting rules without hard-coding the names Ahmed, Rahma, and Mamti into the business logic.

## Game session flow

A typical game session should work as follows:

1. A player creates a game session.
1'. A session code got generated.
2. The three players join using that session code or are added to the session by their IDs.
3. The application determines the first player, potentially using the youngest-player rule or a random selection.
4. Turns proceed in clockwise order.
5. During each turn, the active player draws a card.
6. The application displays the card text, category, instructions, and targeted players.
7. Players answer verbally in person.
8. The active player confirms that the card has been completed.
9. A player may use an available power card.
10. Points may optionally be awarded.
11. The game continues until the selected number of cards has been completed.

The application should support at least two session lengths:

* Short session: approximately 15 cards.
* Long session: approximately 30 cards.

It should also support a custom number of cards.

## Game modes

Please design the architecture to support:

* Mixed mode using cards from all categories.
* Category-specific mode.
* Short game.
* Long game.
* Custom game.
* Optional scoring mode.
* Relaxed mode without scoring.
* Local game, where all players use one device.
* Future online multiplayer mode, where each player uses their own device.

For the first version, local single-device gameplay is sufficient, but the database and backend design should not prevent future online multiplayer support.

## Power cards

At the beginning of a session, each player receives a configurable number of power cards (configurable by the admin), such as two cards.

Each power card can normally be used once per session.

Examples include:

### Whisper

The selected player answers the question by whispering the answer privately to another player.

### Prefer Not to Answer

The player may skip a question without explaining why.

### Mirror

The other players must also answer the same question.

### Full Story

The player must provide a detailed story rather than a short answer.

### Change the Question

The current card is discarded and another card is drawn.

### Everyone Answers

A question intended for one player becomes a question for all players.

### A Word from the Heart

A selected player must say something sincere and positive to another player.

### No Diplomacy

The player should give an honest answer instead of choosing the answer that is most likely to please others.

The system should support adding new power-card types later without major database changes.

Please determine whether power-card effects should be implemented using:

* An enum.
* A strategy pattern.
* A rules engine.
* Configurable JSON data.
* Separate backend handlers.
* Another suitable design.

Explain the advantages and disadvantages of your recommendation.

## Challenge and surprise cards

Challenge cards may ask players to perform actions such as:

* Whispering a positive message.
* Making the others laugh within a limited time.
* Taking a group photo.
* Sharing a light confession.
* Giving compliments.
* Secretly ranking the players according to a personality trait.
* Acting like another player without speaking.
* Dedicating a song to another player.
* Writing a family wish.
* Selecting a family activity for the following week.
* Describing the best moment from the current game session.

Some challenge cards may require:

* A timer.
* Selecting one or more players.
* Awarding points.
* Entering a short text response.
* Taking or selecting a photo.
* Marking a challenge as completed.
* Scheduling or saving an activity for later.

The first version does not necessarily need photo upload or scheduling, but the architecture should allow these features to be added later.

## Game rules

The application should display the following principles before starting a game:

* Players should be honest without being hurtful.
* Players should not judge one another’s answers.
* Answers shared during the game must not be used against a player later.
* Players should not interrupt one another.
* Laughing together is encouraged, but mocking another player is not allowed.
* Every player has the right to skip a sensitive question.
* In guessing cards, the first answer should be kept.
* Disagreement should not become an argument.
* Phones should only be used when required by a challenge.
* If a question causes tension, the players should pause and reconcile before continuing.

## Golden rule

The game is not designed to test love or determine who is the best player.

Its goal is to make every player feel heard, loved, respected, and secure within the family.

If the players laugh, speak honestly, and discover something new about one another, then all three players have won.

## Optional scoring system

The game should work with or without scores.

A player may receive points when they:

* Correctly predict another player’s answer.
* Make the other players laugh.
* Remember an old detail that others had forgotten.
* Give an especially honest or meaningful answer.
* Complete a challenge successfully.

At the end of a scored session, the player with the highest score may select something such as:

* The next meal.
* The next movie.
* The next family outing.
* The group photo.
* The next family challenge.

The data model should support multiple scoring events during one card turn and should save the reason for every awarded point.

## Offline behavior

Because the three players may play together using one phone, the game should ideally continue if the internet connection is temporarily unavailable.

Please recommend one of the following approaches, or a better alternative:

* A fully local Flutter game with optional cloud synchronization.
* A Spring Boot backend required for every action.
* An offline-first application using a local Flutter database and background synchronization.
* A hybrid model where public card content is cached locally while sessions are synchronized when connectivity is available.

Explain which approach is most appropriate for the first version and how conflicts should be handled.
